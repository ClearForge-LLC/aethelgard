## 1. USB 2.0 full-speed — READ-PRIMARY
Document: "Universal Serial Bus Specification, Revision 2.0, April 27, 2000" (the usb_20.pdf inside https://www.usb.org/sites/default/files/usb_20_20250603.zip, folder usb_20_20240927; ECN PDFs are separate files in the zip and were NOT checked for amendments to these sections). Text extracted to usb2/usb20.txt.
(a) Impedance. Sec 7.1.1.1 (printed p.128): "A full-speed USB connection is made through a shielded, twisted pair cable with a differential characteristic impedance (Z0) of 90 Ω ±15%, a common mode impedance (ZCM) of 30 Ω ±30%, and a maximum one-way delay (TFSCBL) of 26 ns."
 Table 7-12 (printed p.185) "Differential Cable Impedance ZO (90 Ω ±15%); 76.5 min, 103.5 max Ω (full-/high-speed)".
 PCB: Sec 7.1.6.1 (printed p.142): "The transmission line between the receptacle and RS must be 90 Ω ±15%." (context: CIND 150 pF = up to 75 pF lumped at transceiver+connector and "an additional 75 pF capacitance on each conductor in the transmission line between the receptacle and the transceiver" for downstream ports; upstream device with detachable cable CINUB 100 pF = 75 pF lumped + "an additional 25 pF capacitance on each conductor in the transmission line between the receptacle and the transceiver").
 So the spec does state a 90 Ω ±15% requirement on the board trace between receptacle and RS for FS (7.1.6.1). The explicit "nominal differential impedance of 90 Ω" board-trace sentence is in the HS section 7.1.1.3 (printed p.129): "The D+ and D- circuit board traces which run between a transceiver and its associated connector should also have a nominal differential impedance of 90 Ω, and together they may add an additional 4 ns of delay between the transceivers."
(b) Rise/fall. Table 7-9 "Full-speed Source Electrical Characteristics" (printed p.181; NB: in this revision Table 7-8 is the HIGH-speed table): "Rise Time TFR Figure 7-8; Figure 7-9 min 4 max 20 ns" / "Fall Time TFF Figure 7-8; Figure 7-9 4 20 ns" / "Differential Rise and Fall Time Matching TFRFM (TFR/TFF) Note 10, Section 7.1.2: 90 – 111.11 %".
 Sec 7.1.2.1 (printed p.130): "the output rise time and fall times are measured between 10% and 90% of the signal (Figure 7-8)... The rise and fall times for full-speed buffers are measured with the load shown in Figure 7-9. The rise and fall times must be between 4 ns and 20 ns and matched to within ±10%". Figure 7-9: "CL = 50pF" (each of D+ and D-, via RS). Crossover VCRS 1.3–2.0 V.
(c) Driver impedance. Table 7-9: "Driver Output Resistance for driver which is not high-speed capable ZDRV, Section 7.1.1.1: 28 min, 44 max Ω". Sec 7.1.1.1: "When the full-speed driver is not part of a high-speed capable transceiver, the impedance of each of the drivers (ZDRV) must be between 28 Ω and 44 Ω... When the full-speed driver is part of a high-speed capable transceiver, the impedance of each of the drivers (ZHSDRV) must be between 40.5 Ω and 49.5 Ω". (Note under 7.1.1.3: HS-capable transceiver in FS/LS mode "ZHSDRV of 45 Ω ±10%".)

## 3. Closed-form coupled microstrip
- IPC-2141A (2004) is paywalled; NOT read. Formula text below is 2ND (web calculators/blogs), not a citation:
  Z0 = 87/sqrt(εr+1.41) · ln(5.98h/(0.8w+t))  [prototypeguru.com/microstrip-impedance-calculator-ipc-2141/ : "Z₀ = (87 / √(Er + 1.41)) × ln(5.98 × H / (0.8W + T))"; engicalchub.com same].
  Differential: nrtechstudio.com/tools/pcb-differential-impedance-calculator/ : "k = 0.48 x exp( -0.96 x s / h )" and "Zdiff = 2 x Z0 x (1 - k)"; trafolo.eu: "The 0.48/0.96 coupling fit is an empirical curve from IPC-2141, not a field solution. For tight coupling (s/h below about 0.5) it can be several ohms out". (Search summary noted one calculator writes S/W instead of S/H; the others use h.)
  Validity (2ND): f4inx.github.io/posts/microstrip-formulas-comparison.html: "0.1<w/h<2.0 and 1<epsilon_r<15" (citing ref [28]); same page: Fig.5 IPC-2141 single-microstrip error "up to 44 %" (mostly u>2), "less than 2.1 %" for narrower range; "range of validity is extremely narrow". Calculator sites claim ±5–7% (prototypeguru). Treat the accuracy as unestablished until IPC-2141A itself is read.
- Wadell, Transmission Line Design Handbook (1991): book not obtained. Only Altium's page (resources.altium.com/p/clearing-up-trace-impedance-calculators-and-formulas) was read: it says Wadell's Wheeler-based microstrip equations are more accurate than IPC-2141 ("less than 0.7% error" per search snippet; page text itself did not state a figure I could quote). No Wadell coupled-microstrip formula text or validity range obtained. UNREACHED.
- Kirschning & Jansen, IEEE Trans. MTT-32(1) 83–90, Jan 1984, DOI 10.1109/TMTT.1984.1132616 (+1985 correction). Abstract (READ via search.isc.ac/inventory/49/935380.htm, a metadata mirror): "closed-form expressions ... which model the frequency-dependent even- and odd-mode characteristics of parallel coupled microstrip lines with hitherto unattained accuracy and range of validity... The formulas are accurate into the millimeter-wave region." The abstract gives NO numeric accuracy or range.
  Numeric claims are 2ND via Qucs technical documentation (qucs.github.io/tech/node77.html, "Parallel coupled microstrip lines"): applicability "0.1 ≤ u ≤ 10, 0.1 ≤ g ≤ 10, 1 ≤ εr ≤ 18" (u=W/h, g=s/h); "The accuracy of the static impedances is better than 0.6%."; dispersion: "Up to fn=25 the maximum error of these equations is 1.4%" (even-mode eps_eff dispersion) and "Up to fn=20 ... Z_L,o(fn) and Z_L,e(fn) is less than 2.5%". (One search summary also saw "0.6% over 0.1≤W/h≤100, 1≤εr≤20" on a different Qucs page; not read.)
- Hammerstad & Jensen 1980 coupled model per Qucs (2ND): "validated in the range 0.1 ≤ u ≤ 10 and g ≥ 0.01".

- Garg & Bahl, IEEE MTT-27, 1979 pp.700–705 ("Characteristics of coupled microstriplines") and Gupta et al. "Microstrip Lines and Slotlines" 2nd ed. are the likely table/graph sources; neither obtained. No Z0e/Z0o numeric rows from any published table were read.
- Hammerstad & Jensen, "Accurate models for microstrip computer-aided design", IEEE MTT-S Int. Microwave Symp. Dig., 1980, pp.407–409, DOI 10.1109/MWSYM.1980.1124303. Paper not read (IEEE/scilit blocked).
  Single homogeneous microstrip formula, as printed in Qucs technical docs (2ND; qucs.github.io/tech/node75.html, eq. 11.4–11.6): ZL1(W,h) = ZF0/(2π) · ln( fu·h/W + sqrt(1+(2h/W)^2) ), fu = 6 + (2π−6)·exp(−(30.666·h/W)^0.7528), ZL = ZL1/sqrt(εeff) (the εr=1 case gives ZL1). Qucs states of this model: "better than 0.01% for W/h ≤ 1 and 0.03% for W/h ≤ 1000" — this is Qucs's relaying of the paper's claim for the homogeneous-medium Z (accuracy of the closed form vs. the exact integral), not a claim about real FR-4 boards. Another site (rfessentials.com, 2ND) says "±0.2% for εeff and ±1% for Z0 when 0.1<W/h<100"; the two disagree on what is claimed — resolve by reading the paper.
  f4inx page (2ND): "All other formulas were found to have an accuracy better than 1.5 %" relative to H&J; H&J "believed to be the most accurate".
