# Aethelgard: independent review (Mythos)

Reviewed commit `5b46b862851e6a44bd57e5bd124aee55ff7059ea` (`origin/main`, 2026-11-12), in `~/aethelgard-review`, read-only.

## Executive summary

1. The engineering discipline is real: fail-closed exits, two-witness judging, a sandbox with no escape hatch, strict archive decoding, and attribution that refuses to guess. I could not break any of those.
2. The largest gap is between the one-sentence claim and what the gates see. **The fault campaign never sees the board.** It computes R × C from two numbers the specification declares (A1).
3. Reproduced: the campaign passes 24 Ω, 50 Ω and 100 Ω pull-ups, 47 kΩ with a declared 5 pF, and 10 MΩ with a declared 1 fF (A1, A2).
4. **The installed binary cannot generate the board crate, the firmware or the Renode platform.** Only a test with `AETHELGARD_BLESS=1` writes them (A3).
5. **No verdict binds the gates to one pair** (`INV-03`). `check drc --spec` passes a board against an unrelated specification, and `prove` passes a vacuous hand-written harness (A4).
6. "Rules: specification" in a DRC verdict is a constant 0.2 mm floor; the specification is not read (A5, already recorded, unowned).
7. The README's status is two phases stale, and its quickstart CI tests a published image from 2026-11-08 that lacks `campaign`, `replay` and `attribute` (A6).
8. Tests are strong overall. Three mutants I applied in a copy show the holes: two survived (A7).
9. Roughly 20 "carried forward" findings have no owning work order (A9).
10. Top three: one `verify` command that regenerates and gates everything; an honest electrical gate (static rules now, extraction before any fabrication package); close the test-tier and quickstart gaps before P4.
11. A second, bounded round on the sandbox, MCP argument handling, the archive reader and the boot judge found no bypass; it found one medium lead (a firmware could forge the I2C witness, A11.1) and several low ones. Not reviewed: the KiCad reader and parity code, the router, the board-crate generator.

## A1. The electrical gate judges declared numbers, never the board — high; the design is recorded, the consequence is not

- **Evidence (read).** `crates/aethelgard-ir/src/solve.rs:294-297` copies the bus's `pull_up_ohms` and the net constraint's `max_capacitance_femtofarads` into `analog_lines`. `crates/aethelgard-twin/src/analog.rs:93-104` builds the whole netlist from those two numbers, the rail, and an ideal switch. Nothing reads the emitted `.kicad_pcb`. `AET-WO-1303` (extraction) is reserved, unbuilt.
- **Evidence (run).** `aethelgard campaign` over five `expect: pass` scenarios on the reference specification:

  | Scenario | Overrides | Verdict |
  |---|---|---|
  | `r47k-c5p` | 47 kΩ, both nets' `max_capacitance_femtofarads` 5000 | pass |
  | `r10M-c1f` | 10 MΩ, 1 fF | pass |

  Campaign line: `{"ok":true,"verdict":"pass","check":"campaign","scenarios":5,"matched":5,…}`, exit 0.
- **What it means.**
  - The P2 gate's "47 kΩ fails" is a function of the declared 100 pF, a number a person typed. `max_capacitance` is named as a bound and used as the measurement.
  - The archive records call this `source: "ngspice"` and `analog.rise_time_measured`. ngspice is evaluating 0.847 × R × C.
  - The northstar's sentence says the pair is judged together. Pins and nets do cross, through the shared `SolvedTopology` and schematic parity. No physical property of the routed board reaches the campaign.
- **Already recorded.** `AET-WO-2200.md:33` says the values come from the specification, and its FEEDBACK A8 notes a 1 fF line (`AET-WO-2200.FEEDBACK.md:143`). I found no document that draws the consequence for the P2 gate's claim or for `INV-07`.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Recommendation.**
  - Say so in the README and in the record's wording now ("computed from declared values").
  - Build `AET-WO-1303` before the P3 fabrication-package clause, and judge *extracted ≤ declared* so the declared figure becomes a real constraint (`INV-07`-clean).

## A2. No pull-up floor: a 24 Ω pull-up solves and passes the campaign — high, new

- **Evidence (run).** Same campaign: `r24` (24 Ω), `r50`, `r100` each `"verdict":"pass","matched":true`. `attribute` on each archived run then says `"domain":"none","rule":"N1"`. I did not run `emit` or ERC on these.
- **Evidence (read).** `crates/aethelgard-ir/src/lib.rs:623` refuses only 0. The deck's driver is `ron=10` (`analog.rs:99`), and "stuck high" triggers only above 30 % of the rail, so anything from about 24 Ω up reads as a fast, healthy bus.
- **Why it matters.** At 3.3 V, 24 Ω asks the driver to sink 137 mA. UM10204 allows 3 mA, a floor near 967 Ω. `AET-WO-3200` §1.2 gives that floor to the *proposer* only. A hand-written specification, or a later model proposer (`RUL-18`), gets past the gate; that is the `NG-04` case.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Recommendation.** Put the floor in the gate before 3200 builds: a `solve` refusal, or an analog finding such as `analog_sink_current_exceeded`. The proposer then cites the gate's constant instead of owning it.

## A3. The CLI cannot generate half of what the README says it generates — high, recorded as a deliberate gap whose reason has expired

- **Evidence (read).**
  - The command tree is `validate, solve, emit, check, prove, simulate, campaign, replay, attribute, mcp` (`crates/aethelgard-cli/src/main.rs:55-140`). `emit` writes KiCad files only.
  - `grep` over `crates/aethelgard-cli/src`: the only `aethelgard_svd_hal` uses are `pin_table` and `cmsis` (`main.rs:641,1094`). `board::generate` is never called.
  - The board crate, firmware and `.repl` reach disk through `AETHELGARD_BLESS=1 cargo test -p aethelgard-svd-hal --test board` (`crates/aethelgard-svd-hal/tests/board.rs:11,111`).
  - `simulate --firmware --platform` requires a byte-identical emitted `.repl`, which no subcommand emits. The CLI README (line 204) says it is "committed for the reference board".
- **Already recorded.** Roadmap §7 (the `0300` row, and the P0 gate row at `roadmap.md:331`): "The one deliberate gap is still the `build` subcommand", because "nothing generates until `AET-WO-1300`". Generators have existed since 1400 and 2100, and no work order picks the gap up.
- **Against.** `README.md:5-11`: "From one specification, `aethelgard` … generates a typestate board crate … boots generated firmware".
- **Consequence.** A user with their own specification gets a schematic and board and stops. `INV-01`'s falsifier ("regenerate and diff") can be run only by someone with the repository and its test harness.
- **Confidence:** high.
- **Recommendation.** Extend `emit` (or add `generate`) to write the board crate, firmware crate and platform. It is a thin call over existing library functions. See B1.

## A11. Archive reader and boot judge (second subagent round; I spot-checked the cited lines)

- **A11.1 A hand-written firmware can probably pass with no I2C transaction — medium, read only, partly recorded.**
  - **Evidence.** The witness compares only `CR2`'s `SADD`, `RD_WRN`, `NBYTES` and `AUTOEND` fields and `TXDR`'s low byte (`crates/aethelgard-twin/src/boot.rs:183-195`, `:294-324`). `START` is outside the mask. The reference boot leaves `CR2 = 0x020104EC`, `TXDR = 0xD0` (`AET-WO-2202.FEEDBACK.md:60`), constants a firmware can write directly, beside `STATUS_OK` and `0x60` in the record.
  - **Recorded.** 2202's forged-record test writes the record only and "never calls `transfer`"; a forger that also writes the two registers is not covered. The CLI README (line 251) says only a transaction leaves the controller in that state.
  - **Confidence:** medium. Whether Renode stores a `TXDR` write outside a transfer was not measured.
  - **Recommendation.** Measure it in the tools image. Either add an observable a plain write cannot set (`ISR`, `RXDR`), or state that the witness guards a mistaken generated firmware, not a hostile one.
- **A11.2 The manifest is read with no size cap — low, read only, new.** `crates/aethelgard-chaos/src/archive.rs:218-223,300`: `read_to_end` on `manifest.cbor` before any bound; the trace is bounded, the manifest is not. A sparse multi-gigabyte manifest in a hostile run directory would be allocated. Confidence high from the code; not run, to protect the host. Recommendation: `take(1 MiB)`.
- **A11.3 State is built before the run rules bound it — low, unverified estimate.** Up to 256 MiB of minimal events are folded into `State.records` (`replay.rs:59-61`) before `check_archived` refuses the run. Recommendation: refuse a second scenario, topology, boot or verdict record at fold time.
- **Held (run by the subagent, five inputs, all exit 3):** `trace.cbor` as a symlink (`UnexpectedEntry`); `manifest.cbor` as a FIFO (`UnexpectedEntry`, no hang); a resealed trace with a 65,535-element array (`MalformedEvent`); a manifest declaring 2^64−1 trace bytes (`TraceTooLarge`); a resealed trace with one trailing byte (`MalformedEvent`).

# Part B: collaboration

## What I would not change

- **The exit taxonomy.** "Fail" (1), "cannot run" (4) and "cannot trust the output" (5) are distinct, and nothing maps doubt to a pass.
- **Copy, then hash, then judge the copy**, and two witnesses for every tool.
- **The sandbox having no flag that turns it off.**
- **Attribution's shape:** closed rules, positive evidence, `indeterminate` as a first-class answer.
- **`RUL-16`'s two types**, and the strict integer-only archive.
- **§2 of `architecture.md`:** measurements with the wrong belief kept beside the correction. This is the project's best habit.
- **The adversarial pass and FEEDBACK cadence.** Most of what I went looking for had already been found and closed.

## Roadmap order

- **Do `2301` (dynamic faults) before `4500`, and say so.** A flight controller's field failures are brownouts and interrupt storms, both named in the northstar's problem statement and neither simulated yet. P4's gate asks only for a *static* campaign. Opinion.
- **There is no power model at all.** The northstar's "brownout the regulator permits is one the firmware recovers from" has no work order before `4300`'s "power input and regulator parts". Consider a rail-droop spike inside `2301`.
- **Run the Nucleo bench comparison early in P3**, before `3200`'s proposals are trusted. It is the only source of evidence about A1, and the board is already ordered.
- **`4100` (stackups) could follow `4200` (SPI end to end).** SPI devices are needed by every P4 gate clause; four layers are needed only by the last.

## For `AET-WO-3200` specifically

- Fix the two wrong premises in A8 before kickoff, or the builder stops on them.
- The re-gate (§1.4) needs the board crate for `proposed-spec.json`. With A3 open, only a test can make one. B1 would let the re-gate be a single command.
- A proposal that lowers the pull-up should be re-checked against B2's floor *by the gate*, so the 0.8 headroom and the floor are not two facts in two places.

# Part C: top three

1. **Build `aethelgard verify`, with generation in the CLI.** One command that regenerates the schematic, board, board crate, firmware and platform from a specification, runs ERC, DRC with parity, the proof and the campaign on exactly those bytes, and prints one verdict naming every hash. It turns `INV-01` and `INV-03` from conventions kept by repository tests into something a user can run, closes A3 and A4 together, and is the verdict the P3 fabrication-package clause already assumes exists.

2. **Make the electrical gate say what it knows.** Today it computes a rise time from a declared resistance and a declared capacitance bound, and has no lower limit on the pull-up. Add the static rules now (B2, small), relabel the records as computed from declared values, and bring routed-board capacitance into the judgement before any fabrication package is called verified (B3). Until then, "survived simulated electrical faults together" is true of the specification and the firmware, not of the board.

3. **Close the evidence gaps in CI before P4 adds surface.** A canary proving tier 2 ran, the quickstart run against `main`'s own image, the missing-key test over every key, and one owned backlog table. Each is small. Together they protect the thing this project does best, which is knowing whether a claim was checked.
