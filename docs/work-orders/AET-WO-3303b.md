# AET-WO-3303b — The schedule harness split on every board, not only on a board with a timer

**Repo:** `ClearForge-LLC/aethelgard` · **Base:** `main`, named in the kickoff.
**Branch:** `wo/aet-3303b-split-every-board`.
**Author:** Claude (architect) · **Builder:** strong tier, with a full adversarial pass.
**Phase:** P3 (`3303`'s split, finished). Written 2026-11-19. **Cadence:** build, with one measured stop.

**Grounds:**
- **`3303` split the whole-schedule Kani harness** into rely–guarantee parts: one per I2C or SPI peripheral, one per timer, and one for unowned GPIO, refused by `check_parts` when the parts' conjunction is not the whole claim.
  - The roadmap records it as "the whole-schedule harness is split per peripheral and timer".
- **That holds only on a board with a timer.** `emit_proofs` in `crates/aethelgard-svd-hal/src/board.rs` (about L3897 on `main`) emits the whole harness unsplit when `plan.timers.is_empty()`. The comment there says "byte for byte as before".
  - So Sentinel-01 proves its schedule whole (`boards/sentinel-01/src/proofs.rs` L407), and so does every other board without motor outputs.
- **What that costs** (`4600` F11, measured in the pinned image):
  - I2C only: 110.7 s and 4.62 GB.
  - Two SPI buses, no I2C: killed at 900‰ of an 8 GiB cap at 417 s. At 16 GiB: 390 s and 10.65 GB.
  - I2C plus SPI: OOM at 8 GiB after 1,032 s. At 16 GiB: 834.5 s and 13.00 GB.
  - Each timer-split part on the motor driver: at most 2.49 GB.
  - `4201` F4 measured an OOM at 8 GiB on Sentinel-01 itself. `3304` F3 measured 9.1 GiB.
- **Ruled on #245 (comment 7020363017):** `prove` runs at `--mem 16G` on any board without a timer until this work order says otherwise from measurement.

> **What this is:**
> - the schedule harness split into `parts()` on every board where the split yields two or more parts, with `check_parts` holding the same conjunction rule;
> - the cost measured before and after, per harness, on every board in `boards/` and on two no-timer boards from `4600`'s record;
> - a recommendation, from those numbers, for the memory cap local callers need.
>
> It is NOT:
> - a change to what any harness claims. The conjunction of the parts is the whole claim, or `check_parts` refuses;
> - a change to the specification, the IR, the twin or the generated firmware;
> - the shard count. That is `1911`'s.

## 1. Build

1. **Measure first.** On `main`, before any change, measure `prove` for each harness of each board:
   - every board under `boards/`;
   - `spike/4600/logger-i2c-only.json` and `spike/4600/logger-spi-only-cap.json`, copied to your scratch and re-pinned to `main`'s schema version. Change nothing else in them, and say so.

   Record wall time and peak memory (`capped-run`'s scope peak) at `--mem 16G`, in a table in FEEDBACK. Write `PREDICTIONS.md` in `spike/3303b/` **before** the after-runs, and commit it on its own: each part's expected time and peak, and the total.
2. **The split.**
   - `emit_proofs` splits whenever `parts(plan)` yields two or more parts, timers or not. A board with one part keeps the whole harness: a split into one part is the whole.
   - `parts()`, `Scope::actions` and `check_parts` work unchanged on a board with no timer, or you say exactly what had to change and why.
   - **The reset-clock read with no timer:** each peripheral part schedules it. State in FEEDBACK how `check_parts` treats it as one owner across parts, as it does for a port enable and the kernel selection, and cite the line.
   - `verify` and `prove`: name the code that chooses which harnesses run (by name, or by enumeration). Show that a no-timer board's split harnesses all run under `verify`, and that `proofs::every_valid_sequence` no longer exists as a proof there, or still runs, whichever is true. A harness that silently drops out is this work order's failure.
3. **Tests.**
   - A tier-1 test on a no-timer plan with two I2C peripherals and one SPI. Its parts are emitted, `check_parts` passes, and each part's scope is what `parts()` documents.
   - **A mutant:** a no-timer plan whose two parts' footprints share a bit. `check_parts` refuses it by name (`UnscheduledAction` or the overlap error).
   - **A mutant:** an action of the whole schedule that no part schedules, on a no-timer board. Refused.
   - Generated boards under `boards/` are regenerated by the repository's own tooling, never by hand. Goldens are updated by the tooling that owns them.
4. **CI weights.** `1910`'s planner refuses a stale weight row (P1) and an unweighted heavy test (P2). Re-weigh every test whose cost this moves, with the method `ci/test-weights.tsv` documents, and show the planner silent on the new rows.
   - If `1911` has merged, merge `main` first, and weigh against its shard layout.
   - If it has not, say so on the bus. The architect sequences the merges.
5. **The cap.** From §1.1's after-numbers, recommend the `--mem` cap local callers need for `prove` and for `verify` on a board without a timer. State the largest peak measured, which board, and which harness. The ruling is the architect's.

## 5. Adversarial pass, full

- **A split whose conjunction is weaker than the whole:**
  - an action no part schedules;
  - a shared bit with two owners;
  - an ordering between parts that the whole harness constrained and no part does.

  Name each, and say which check or test catches it.
- **A harness that no longer runs** under `verify` or CI and nobody notices.
- **A peak measured on a run that was not the split's:** a cached proof, or the whole harness still running beside the parts.
- **Weights fitted to one noisy run.**

## 7. Flag-and-stop conditions

- **A part fails where the whole passed, or the whole fails where every part passes.** Stop with both outputs. That is the claim changing.
- **`check_parts` refuses a board under `boards/` or one of the two `4600` boards.** Stop with the refusal.
- **Any part's peak exceeds 8 GiB.** Not a stop: record it, and let it set §1.5's recommendation.
- **Running the parts needs a change in `cli`'s harness selection.** Stop and say which lines.
